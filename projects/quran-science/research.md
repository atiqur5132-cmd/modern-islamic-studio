## The one idea

Three questions let anyone read a verse that people connect with science: the word, the early
scholars, then the science. Asked in that order, they show what the verse really gives (a promise,
a blessing, justice, an oath, a reflection), and science adds colour without being forced onto it.

1. **Lafz:** what does the Arabic word mean, and where else does the Quran use it?
2. **Ulama:** how did the early scholars, who spoke Arabic as their own language, understand it?
3. **Science:** what does science actually say, and was it already known before?

The viewer leaves with the test, not just five verdicts. That makes the video worth sharing.

Picture to hold: a stamp. Each chapter ends with one verdict stamp (`TRUE, BUT…`, `FALSE`,
`NUMBERS MATCH, MEANING DOESN'T`), and the three questions sit in a corner card that ticks
through for every claim.

## Chapter research

### Ch 1. Fingerprints (75:3–4)

- **Lafz:** *banan* = fingertips (both translations). The verse names the fingertips, not the
  lines on them.
- **Context:** resurrection. Ma'ariful Quran: deniers asked how dust and bones could live again; the
  verse answers that Allah can rebuild even the smallest part.
- **Ulama (classical):** Tabari on 75:4 reports Ibn Abbas, by several chains: "لو شاء لجعله خفا أو
  حافرا" (if He willed He would make it a camel's foot or a hoof), also Ikrimah ("like a camel's
  foot or a donkey's hoof"). Ibn Kathir: "We could make his fingertips all equal". So the classical
  wonder is the separate, working fingers.
- **Ulama (modern):** Ma'ariful Quran (Mufti Muhammad Shafi, 20th c.) adds the fingerprint reading:
  "No two thumbprints or fingerprints are alike", and "Thumbprints have played a decisive role in
  court decisions". He gives it as a second reason, after the smallness of the fingertips.
- **Science:** Wikipedia "Fingerprint" (read 2026-11-11): "Even identical twins (who share their DNA)
  do not have identical fingerprints." Galton's book *Finger Prints* (1892). The Henry
  classification system "was developed in India"; the Kolkata fingerprint bureau "was established in
  1897"; Scotland Yard began identifying criminals by fingerprints in 1901.
- **Verdict:** `TRUE, BUT…`: fingerprints really are unique, and a respected tafsir draws the link;
  the verse does not predict them. It is a reflection the verse allows.

### Ch 2. Two seas (55:19–20, 25:53)

- **The video:** the famous photo of a line between light and dark blue water was taken by Ken
  Bruland (ocean sciences, UC Santa Cruz) on a 2007 research cruise in the Gulf of Alaska. It shows
  sediment-laden water from glacial rivers meeting the open ocean, not two oceans. Bruland: "They do
  eventually mix, but you do come across these really strong gradients at these specific moments in
  time." (Alaska Dispatch News, Ben Anderson, 5 Feb 2013.) The borders move and disappear. A
  second photo of this kind is by Kent Smith (July 2010). PolitiFact (2021) and USA Today/Yahoo
  rated the "two oceans that don't mix" caption false.
- **Lafz:** *bahrayn* = two seas, but 25:53 itself says which: "one fresh and sweet, one salty and
  bitter". So *bahr* here covers a body of fresh water too. This comes from the Quran's own wording,
  no lexicon needed.
- **Ulama:** Ibn Kathir on 55:19: "The two seas are the fresh and salty waters, the former coming
  from running rivers", and the barrier is "a barrier of land". Tabari on 55:20: *barzakh* is anything
  between two things; Qatada: "this island, this dry land"; Mujahid: "a barrier from Allah". Qurtubi
  lists: the land between (Hasan, Qatada), Divine power, and others. Ma'ariful Quran: where sweet
  and salty waters meet "there is a distinct barrier", samples "in every region of the world".
- **Science:** NOAA (oceanservice.noaa.gov, estuaries tutorial, circulation page): river water
  "often floats on top of the heavier seawater"; in salt-wedge estuaries "fresh water floating on top
  and a wedge of saltwater on the bottom"; "Some mixing does occur at the boundary… but it is
  generally slight", and mixing depends on wind, tide, river flow and the estuary's shape.
- **Verdict:** the video is `FALSE` (two oceans, never mix). The verse is about sweet and salt
  water side by side, which sailors and fishermen always saw. The video made the verse smaller than
  it is: the meaning is a blessing (drinkable water beside the sea), hence 55:21's refrain.

### Ch 3. Iron (57:25) and the numbers

- **The claim:** "sent down" means iron came from space; science now says iron is made in stars
  (Harun Yahya is the main source, per WikiIslam's critique, search result only). Numerical add-on:
  Surah al-Hadid is number 57; the abjad value of الحديد is 57, of حديد is 26; iron's atomic number is
  26; and "iron-57" is an isotope.
- **Lafz:** the same verb *anzala* is used for cattle (39:6, "eight pairs of the cattle") and for
  clothing (7:26).
- **Ulama:** Ma'ariful Quran on 57:25: "The verb 'sent down' in relation to the iron stands for
  'created'", and in 39:6 "the verb anzala… is unanimously used in the sense of khalaqa" (citing Ruh
  al-Ma'ani). The other side: Qurtubi on 57:25 reports, from Ibn Abbas via Ikrimah (cited from
  al-Mawardi) and via al-Tha'labi, that Adam came down with blacksmith's tools (anvil, tongs, hammer,
  needle). Qurtubi also quotes a narration from Umar that Allah sent down four blessings from the sky:
  iron, fire, water and salt. Neither report's grading was checked; label them "kuch riwayatein"
  (later reports), and they speak of tools, not of stars.
- **Science:** NASA Imagine the Universe ("What is Your Cosmic Connection to the Elements?"): large
  stars fuse helium into carbon and oxygen; "Fusion continues in red supergiants until iron is
  formed." NASA Cosmicopia (archived): "most of the elements in the universe heavier than helium are
  created, or synthesized, in stars". So carbon and oxygen came from stars as well; the claim proves
  too much.
- **Iron from the sky, for real:** Comelli et al., *Meteoritics & Planetary Science* 2016:
  Tutankhamun's dagger blade has more than 10% nickel; iron from ores before the 19th century has
  less than 4%; meteorites 10–30% (Eos, 1 June 2016). A true and interesting fact, not the verse's claim.
- **Numbers (recomputed by us):**
  - حديد: ح 8 + د 4 + ي 10 + د 4 = **26**. Add ال: ا 1 + ل 30 = 31. 26 + 31 = **57**. Arithmetic correct.
  - Iron: atomic number 26 (WebElements). Natural abundance (IUPAC via WebElements/KAERI): Fe-54
    5.845%, **Fe-56 91.754%**, **Fe-57 2.119%**, Fe-58 0.282%. Relative atomic mass 55.845.
  - So "iron is 57" is wrong: the iron in the world is 92% iron-56. The 26 matches; it is a
    numerical observation, chosen after the fact (abjad is one letter-number system among others,
    and the same method finds nothing for other metals per WikiIslam's critique, not opened).
- **Verdict:** `NUMBERS MATCH, MEANING DOESN'T`: the 26 is a coincidence, the 57 is wrong, and
  "sent down" is used for cattle and clothes. The verse's point (Ma'ariful Quran): Book, balance and
  iron are given so that people uphold justice; iron is power, and power must serve justice.

### Ch 4. Black hole (81:15–16)

All from `projects/khunnas-black-hole/research.md` (ledger rows 2–11): *khunnas* shares the root
kh-n-s with *khannas* (114:4), "to draw back"; *kunnas* as a deer goes into its den (*kinas*);
Ali: stars that hide by day and appear at night; Tabari, Qurtubi and Ma'ariful Quran: the five
planets that seem to move backwards; Ibn Mas'ud: wild cows; retrograde motion (NASA APOD
2010-06-13), known to Greek astronomers (Apollonius); NASA: black holes give no light and are not
seen directly. 81:17–18 swear by the night and the dawn. Verdict `FALSE`.

### Ch 5. Flat earth (88:20)

All from `projects/flat-earth/research.md` (ledger rows 2–6, 12, 14): *sutihat* = levelled, spread;
88:17–20 is a desert traveller's view; Ibn Atiyya and Tafsir al-Jalalayn read it as flat; Razi: a
huge sphere looks flat from any spot on it; Ibn Hazm (d. 456 AH / 1064 CE): the earth is round and
no imam denied it. Verdict `FALSE` for the critics' claim too.

### Payoff. The ant (27:18–19)

From `projects/naml-ant-warning/research.md` (rows 3, 5–8, 11): E. O. Wilson 1958, one gland's
alarm scent alerts ants 5 cm away in 4–9 s and spreads through the nest; 27:18's "wa hum la
yash'urun"; Qurtubi: the ant's words show Sulaiman's justice. The verse does not predict pheromones:
ants scattering at danger was visible to anyone.

### Closing. Why forced claims hurt

- Science changes; tying a verse to today's theory ties it to tomorrow's revision. This is the
  standard Muslim scholarly objection. Sources: Ziauddin Sardar, "Weird science", New Statesman, 21
  Aug 2008 (opened; he is very blunt about i'jaz claims); Nidhal Guessoum, *Islam's Quantum Question*
  (2011), per reviews in *Zygon* (not opened: blocked). Classical precedent often cited: al-Shatibi
  (d. 790 AH / 1388 CE), *al-Muwafaqat*, reading the Quran by what its first Arab hearers knew
  (secondary sources only; some modern scholars disagree with him). **None of these names go in the
  script until a primary page is opened.** The script says it in our own words.
