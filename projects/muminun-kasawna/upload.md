Caption (with line 1: 1,585 characters, under Instagram's 2,200; the first line is 82):

The Quran didn't say Allah "joined" flesh to the bones. It says He clothed them. 🤍

Surah Al-Mu'minun 23:12–14 tells the story of every human body in a few words, each one a picture:

💧 nutfah: a drop
🔗 ʿalaqah: that which clings (classical scholars like Ibn Kathir also read it as a clot of blood)
🤲 mudghah: a small piece of flesh, with no shape yet
🦴 ʿizām: bones
🧥 fa-kasawnā al-ʿizāma laḥmā: "then We clothed the bones with flesh"

Kasawnā comes from the root k-s-w, the same root as kiswah, clothing. Like a mother dressing her child.

Science note: the embryo attaches to the wall of the womb around day 6–7. Reading ʿalaqah this way is an interpretation, not the classical one. And bones and muscles actually form at about the same time, so this verse isn't a timetable. It's a picture of care. The Greek doctor Galen also described the embryo growing in stages, centuries before the Quran.

Then: khalqan ākhar, "another creation". From one drop, one human. And the verse ends: "So blessed is Allah, the best of creators."

## Pinned first comment (both platforms)

The words of 23:12–14, one by one: nutfah (a drop) → ʿalaqah (that which clings / a clot of blood) → mudghah (a small piece of flesh) → ʿizām (bones) → fa-kasawnā al-ʿizāma laḥmā (then We clothed the bones with flesh) → khalqan ākhar (another creation). Many scholars, Ibn Kathir among them, explain "another creation" as the soul being breathed in. Linking ʿalaqah to the embryo attaching to the womb is a modern reading; the classical reading is a clot of blood. Both are shared here, so you can see the difference.
