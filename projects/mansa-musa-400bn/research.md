# Research: "$400 billion: Mansa Musa itihaas ka sabse ameer aadmi?"

Series A, Viral Claim Check (with a Forgotten History spine). Faceless reel: the creator
records the voiceover only; maps, the Catalan Atlas and number graphics carry the picture.
Researched 2026-11-13. The creator reads this before the script.

## The one idea

The $400 billion figure has no source anyone can check. The real evidence of Musa's wealth
is a price: gold got cheaper in Cairo after his Hajj.

## The claim we check

"Mansa Musa, king of Mali, was the richest man in history, worth $400 billion in today's
money." Shared in Hindi as "itihaas ka sabse ameer aadmi" (Hindi pages and AI-short sites
seen in search, 2026-11-13) and in Indian English media (Outlook Business web story,
"surpassed Musk, Bezos & Ambani", seen in search results, not opened).

## The record

| Event | Detail | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hajj | 1324 CE. Arrived outside Cairo July 1324, entered 19 July, left with the Cairo caravan 18 October | Wikipedia, Mansa Musa (verbatim part) |
| Hijri year | Dhu al-Hijjah 724 AH. Our working: tabular Islamic calendar, 1 Muharram 724 = 30 Dec 1323 (Julian); 10 Dhu al-Hijjah 724 ≈ 28 Nov 1324 | computed 2026-11-13 (±1–2 days for sighting) |
| Gold in Cairo | al-Umari (Damascus scholar, wrote in Cairo, late 1330s): "He left no court emir nor holder of a royal office without the gift of a load of gold." The mithqal "did not go below 25 dirhams"; afterwards it "does not exceed 22 dirhams or less"; "about twelve years until this day" | al-Umari, *Masalik al-absar*, tr. J.F.P. Hopkins in Levtzion & Hopkins, *Corpus of Early Arabic Sources for West African History* (CUP 1981), pp. 269–273, via World History Commons |
| Size of the fall | 25 → 22 dirhams = 3/25 = 12% | our working |
| How long | Warren C. Schultz ("Mansa Musa's Gold in Mamluk Cairo", 2006): the twelve-year line is found in one source only; a long disruption "is simply not supportable"; one of many short-term fluctuations. Ibn Kathir records a fall of two dirhams, al-Maqrizi six (via Schultz, not read directly) | Ancient Origins summary of Schultz |
| al-Umari's informant | He was not in Cairo in 1324 (Schultz); Musa befriended the governor Ibn Amir Hajib, whose knowledge passed to al-Umari | Ancient Origins; Wikipedia, Mansa Musa |
| Caravan size | "upwards of 12,000 slaves", 80 camels with 23–136 kg each: figures from later Arabic chroniclers; modern historians call them "rhetorical rather than statistical" (Schultz 2006, Collet 2019) | Wikipedia, Mansa Musa. Not used in the script |
| Money ran out | "Having run out of money, Musa and his entourage were forced to borrow"; debts to merchants such as Siraj al-Din | Wikipedia, Mansa Musa (verbatim). Confirm in Levtzion & Hopkins before final |
| Catalan Atlas | Conventionally dated 1375 (probably late 1370s/early 1380s), by Cresques Abraham, BnF. Musa ("Musse Melly") holds a gold nugget; caption: "This king is the richest and noblest of all these lands." Wikipedia notes this is "frequently misquoted" as an all-time superlative (Hadrien Collet) | Wikipedia, Catalan Atlas (verbatim part) |

## Distances (our working, great-circle)

Timbuktu → Cairo 3,774 km; Cairo → Makkah 1,287 km. Mali's capital in 1324 is uncertain, so
the script says no number; the map shows the route as `ROUTE APPROXIMATE`.
