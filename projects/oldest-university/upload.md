# Upload copy: "Oldest University in the World?" (Nalanda vs al-Qarawiyyin)

English only (channel rule). Titles about 40-55 characters, the search words first, one hook,
no hashtags in the title. Search check (2026-11-17): people type "oldest university in the
world"; the results answer either al-Qarawiyyin or Nalanda, rarely both, so "both are right" is
the angle the crowded results do not offer.

Honesty rules for this copy:
- Nalanda: founded c. 427 CE (a seal names the founder Shakraditya, identified with Kumaragupta I);
  badly damaged by Turkic raids around 1200; in 1235 Dharmasvamin found 2 of 80 viharas in use.
  The chronicle of the time does not name Nalanda, so the copy names no one for its destruction.
- al-Qarawiyyin: founded 859 CE (245 AH) as a mosque in Fez. Fatima al-Fihri's story comes from
  Ibn Abi Zar (14th century), so say "according to tradition". It ran as a madrasa and became a
  modern university in 1963.
- Guinness: "the oldest existing and continually operating educational institution in the world".
  Bologna (1088) is the oldest university in Europe in the degree-giving sense.

## Replies for likely comments

- **"Takshashila was older."** It was an older centre of learning, with teachers and students
  around a city, but not one institution with a campus and residences like Nalanda. That is the
  point of the reel: the answer depends on what you call a university.
- **"Bakhtiyar Khalji destroyed Nalanda."** Turkic raids around 1200 badly damaged Bihar's
  monasteries, and Nalanda with them. The one chronicle of that time describes a fortified vihara
  in Bihar without naming Nalanda, so historians differ on the details. In 1235 a Tibetan monk
  still found two of its 80 viharas in use.
- **"al-Qarawiyyin only became a university in 1963."** True, it ran as a madrasa until then.
  That is why Guinness calls it the oldest continually operating educational institution, and why
  Bologna (1088) holds the record if "university" means a degree-giving body.
- **"What about al-Azhar?"** Founded in 970 in Cairo and still teaching, so it is younger than
  al-Qarawiyyin (859).
- **"Did Fatima al-Fihri really exist?"** Her story comes from a 14th-century history by Ibn Abi
  Zar, written centuries later, and some historians question the details. That is why the reel
  says "kehte hain".
