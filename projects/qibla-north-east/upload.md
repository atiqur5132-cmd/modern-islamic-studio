Why is the qibla in the USA north-east when every map shows Makkah to the south-east? 🧭

In 1953, a new mosque was going up in Washington DC: the Islamic Center of Washington. Its qibla was set at about 56° east of north. People walking past were worried it faced the wrong way. "Take any map… the line would go slightly south," they told The Washington Daily News.

The mosque was right. The map was the problem.

🍊 Peel an orange and try to press the peel flat on a table. It tears in some places and stretches in others. Every flat world map does the same to the round Earth. On the Mercator map we learned in school, Greenland looks as big as Africa, but Africa is 14 times bigger.

🌍 So a straight line on a flat map is not the shortest path on the Earth. Pull a string tight on a globe from Washington to Makkah: it sets off north-east, crosses the Atlantic and Europe, and comes down on Makkah. That first direction is the qibla. The shortest path over a globe is called a great circle.

❄️ From Alaska, the qibla is almost due north, over the top of the world.
📜 Muslim astronomers like al-Biruni were working out the qibla with spherical trigonometry about 1,000 years ago.

Some mosques in North America still face south-east. Many scholars hold that from far away, facing the general direction of the Kaaba is enough, but scholars differ, so for your own prayer, ask a scholar you trust.
