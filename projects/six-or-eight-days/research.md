## The one idea

"Chaar din" in 41:10 is a running total that includes the two days of 41:9, the way
"Delhi se Agra 4 ghante, Lucknow 10 ghante" means 10 hours from Delhi, not 14. So
2 + 2 = 4 for the earth, plus 2 for the heavens = 6.

the days.

## Tafsir and hadith (all opened 2026-11-13)

- **al-Qurtubi on 41:10** (quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/qortobi/sura41-aya10.html):
  "في أربعة أيام يعني في تتمة أربعة أيام. ومثاله قول القائل: خرجت من البصرة إلى بغداد في
  عشرة أيام، وإلى الكوفة في خمسة عشر يوما، أي: في تتمة خمسة عشر يوما. قال معناه ابن
  الأنباري وغيره." "In four days" = completing four days; like saying "I went from Basra to
  Baghdad in ten days and to Kufa in fifteen", i.e. fifteen in all. He gives it from Ibn
  al-Anbari and others. **This is the reel's analogy, in a classical book.**
  - On sawāʾan: al-Hasan: four complete, equal days; al-Farra' (chosen by al-Tabari): the
    provisions were set equally for those in need.
- **Ibn Kathir on 41:10** (quran.ksu.edu.sa/tafseer/katheer/sura41-aya10.html):
  "فهما مع اليومين السابقين أربعة؛ ولهذا قال تعالى: في أربعة أيام": these two with the two
  before make four. On "sawāʾan li-s-sāʾilīn": Ibn Abbas, Qatada and al-Suddi: "لمن أراد
  السؤال عن ذلك ليعلمه", for whoever wants to ask about it, to know. Ibn Zayd: according to
  the need of whoever needs provision. (Ibn Kathir also names weekdays from other reports;
  **not used**.)
- **Sahih al-Bukhari, Kitab al-Tafsir, Surah Ha-Mim al-Sajdah (Fussilat)**: chapter
  narration (hadithunlocked.com/bukhari/65/41; Bukhari gives the chain: Yusuf ibn 'Adi →
  'Ubaydullah ibn 'Amr → Zayd ibn Abi Unaysa → al-Minhal → Sa'id ibn Jubayr). A man told
  Ibn Abbas, "I find things in the Quran that seem to contradict each other", including
  creation verses that put the earth first in one place and the heaven first in another.
  Ibn Abbas: the earth was created in two days, then the heavens in two days, then the
  earth was spread out (water, pasture, mountains) in two days; "so the earth and
  everything in it were created in four days, and the heavens in two days"; and "the Quran
  does not contradict itself, for all of it is from Allah". Sunnah.com did not show this
  chapter text on the page we opened (bukhari:4816); check the sunnah.com chapter wording
  before quoting it on screen.
  - Note: Ibn Abbas's order (earth, heavens, then spreading) differs from the simple
    "2 + 2 then 2" reading; both total six. The reel uses only "kul chaar din zameen, do
    din aasmaan", which both share.

## The other side, stated fairly

Critics (e.g. Answering Islam) argue: the words "fī arbaʿati ayyām" do not by themselves
say "including the previous two", and 41:10 describes work done on an earth already made,
so the days look separate. Fair point about the words alone: read in isolation, the line
could be read as four more days. The reel says so. Two answers, both in the script:
1. Arabic, like Hindi, uses running totals this way (Qurtubi's Basra example, from Ibn
   al-Anbari).
2. Where a line can be read two ways, read it with the author's clear statements: the same
   Quran says "six days" in 7 places. A reading that makes the book contradict itself 7
   times is the weaker reading. (Our own reasoning; label: interpretation.)
