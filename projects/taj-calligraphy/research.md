## Open gap — read first

The hook's fact (letters are larger higher up so they read evenly from the ground) is
repeated everywhere, but **no source we could open gives a measurement or names exactly
which panels.** What we have:

- Pletcher (ed.), *The Geography of India: Sacred and Historic Places*, Britannica
  Educational Publishing 2011, p. 97, as cited by Wikipedia "Amanat Khan Shirazi":
  "Higher panels are written in slightly larger script to reduce the perspective effect
  when viewed from below." (Wikipedia page opened; the book itself not opened.)
- tajmahal.gov.in/calligraphy.aspx (the official Taj Mahal site), opened: "Higher panels
  are written in slightly larger script to reduce the skewing effect when viewed from
  below." No source given.
- The scholarly books that would settle it, Begley & Desai, *Taj Mahal: The Illumined
  Tomb* (1989; chapters "The inscriptions on the Taj Mahal" and "The calligrapher Amanat
  Khan") and Ebba Koch, *The Complete Taj Mahal* (2006), could not be opened: the
  archive.org copy of Begley & Desai is lend-only (needs a free archive.org login and a
  borrow). Begley 1979 (opened in full, below) does not discuss letter size.

So the script says "slightly larger" and nothing more, and its honesty beat says we did
not find the measurement. **If the creator can borrow Begley & Desai on archive.org
(item `tajmahalillumine0000unse`) and check the inscriptions chapter, we can make the
hook stronger or correct it.**
