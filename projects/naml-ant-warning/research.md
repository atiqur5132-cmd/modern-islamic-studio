## The one idea

Science measured the ant's alarm; the Quran, in the same scene, showed the ant's heart. The ant
in Surah An-Naml warns her colony just as real ants do (by scent today's science found), and in
the same breath she does not blame the army: "they will not even know".

Picture for it: a scent "scream" spreading like a ripple through the nest; then the line
"wa hum la yash'urun" ringed on the Ayah card.

## Parked

- "yahtimannakum" (crush) is used for brittle things, and the claim says ant bodies contain glass. Unchecked; likely an overclaim.
- "Namla is female (qalat), and worker ants are female." True biology, but the grammar alone does not decide it, because namla takes a feminine verb for either sex. There is a famous Abu Hanifa–Qatada anecdote; find its source before using it.
- Ka'b's and al-Zamakhshari's details (the valley in Ta'if or Sham, an ant as big as a wolf, heard from three miles) are later reports, partly Isra'iliyyat. Not for the reel.
- Qurtubi: the ant (namla) is named for its constant movement (tanammul).
