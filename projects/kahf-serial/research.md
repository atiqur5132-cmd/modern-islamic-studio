# Kahf Serial: research and part plan

Series D in `SERIES.md`. Status: plan only (2026-11-12). No script, no beats, no assets yet;
each part gets its own `script.txt` and full claim ledger when it is written, and beats only
after its take is recorded.

## The idea in one sentence

Surah al-Kahf tells four stories, and each one is a trap a young person walks into today:
standing alone for your faith, money that feels permanent, "I already know", and power.

The "four trials" (faith, wealth, knowledge, power) is a modern reflective reading, widely
taught today. I found no classical tafsir that names it, so the reels present it as
"ek tareeqa is Surah ko padhne ka" (label: interpretation), never as what the Surah "says".

## Why it can make people wait for the next part

- The Quran itself tells two of the stories with a delay built in. Khidr does three strange
  things (18:71, 18:74, 18:77) and explains them only later (18:79–82). Part 6 ends on the
  three acts; part 7 opens with the reasons. That is a cliffhanger that comes from the
  text, not one we invent.
- A fixed day: every Friday, because reading al-Kahf on Friday is a known practice (Hadith
  below). Viewers know when the next part arrives.
- Numbered and the same look on every part: `KAHF · PART 3/8` on the cover and frame 0.
- Each part still makes sense alone within 3 seconds (a recap line plus picture), so a
  viewer who lands on part 5 goes back for part 1 instead of scrolling away.

## Freshness (web search only, 2026-11-12)

Surah Kahf in Hindi/Urdu on YouTube is long tafsir lectures, recitation with translation,
and dubbed story films (Iranian "Ashab-e-Kahf" episodes: film stills are off limits for us).
The "four trials" framing is common in English blog posts and talks (Yaqeen-style), but I
found no numbered, sourced, visual Hindi shorts serial. A YouTube/Instagram search on the
phone should confirm this before part 1 is shot.

## Part plan (8 parts, about 45–60 s each, one idea per part)

| Part | Story | The one idea | Hook (first 4–5 words carry the key word) | Ends on (cliffhanger) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Cave | The Quran calls them *fityah*, young men (18:10, 18:13): this is a youth story. They left a city where everyone believed otherwise | "Quran ki ek kahaani, jiske hero *teenagers* hain" (wording to be tested) | They fall asleep in the cave. "Kitni der soye? Unhe khud nahi pata tha." |
| 2 | Cave | They wake up thinking it was "a day or part of a day" (18:19); the verse gives 309 years (18:25), then "Allah knows best how long they stayed" (18:26). The silver coin (*wariq*) gives them away | "Ek din soye... ya 309 saal?" | "Agla test us aadmi ka, jise laga uska paisa kabhi khatam nahi hoga." |
| 3 | Two gardens | "I do not think this will ever perish" (18:35): the trap is not having money, it is feeling it is forever | "Mera ye kabhi khatam nahi hoga" | His poorer friend says one sentence to him. "Wo line agle Juma." |
| 4 | Two gardens | The friend's line: *mā shā' Allāh, lā quwwata illā billāh* (18:39), then the garden collapses (18:42) and 18:46 (wealth and children are adornment) | "Ye do lafz jo tum roz bolte ho" (ma sha Allah) | "Agla test: wo insaan jo sabse zyada jaanta tha." |
| 5 | Musa and Khidr | Musa was asked who knows most and said "I do"; Allah told him of a servant who knew more (Bukhari 122). The trap of knowledge is thinking you have all of it | "'Sabse zyada main jaanta hoon'" | The teacher's warning: "Tum mere saath sabr nahi kar paoge" (18:67) |
| 6 | Musa and Khidr | Three acts that look wrong: the boat holed (18:71), the boy (18:74), the wall rebuilt for a town that refused them food (18:77) | "Teen kaam, teeno galat lagte hain" | No reasons given. "Wajah Quran ne baad mein batayi. Agle Juma." (the strongest hook of the serial) |
| 7 | Musa and Khidr | The reasons (18:79–82): the king seizing ships, the parents, the orphans' treasure. What you see is not the whole picture. "I did it not of my own accord" (18:82) | "Kashti mein chhed kyun kiya?" | "Aakhri test: taaqat. Ek raja jiske paas sab kuch tha." |
| 8 | Dhul-Qarnayn | Power used to serve: offered a payment, he says what Allah gave him is better (18:95); builds the barrier and credits it: "This is a mercy from my Lord" (18:98). Close on 18:110 and the four stories in one frame | "Usne paise lene se mana kar diya" | Series end: the four traps in one picture, and what to read this Friday |

Possible bonus (only if the serial works): the first ten verses and the Dajjal hadith,
kept short and calm, not end-times content.

## Hard lines for this serial

- No depiction of Musa, Khidr, or any Prophet or Companion; no faces for the cave youths
  either (they are righteous people of the Quran: treat them the same). Tell the stories
  with objects and places: a silver coin, a cave mouth, a dog's shadow, a garden in time-lapse
  decay, a boat plank, a leaning wall, a fish, a river meeting the sea.
- The cave's location (Ephesus, Amman's al-Raqim, others) and the junction of two seas are
  disputed. Real places can appear as "one place linked to the story" with the dispute said,
  or tagged `RECONSTRUCTION`.
- No film stills or clips from the dubbed Iranian series.
- Stay out of: 18:86 (sun in muddy water; it is its own Viral Claim Check, TOPICS #15),
  Ya'juj and Ma'juj speculation, and Dajjal end-times content.
- Ayah cards via `scripts/fetch_ayah.py` only. The translations above are for checking
  references, not for the screen.
