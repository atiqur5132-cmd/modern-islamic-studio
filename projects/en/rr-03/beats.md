## Look: "The Ledger" (numbers before building)

A warm editorial collage on one big paper board the camera travels across, like a financial newspaper come alive.
Real photos are printed on paper with a white border, a slight tilt and a soft shadow; logos sit on small white cards
beside the person; numbers become animated graphs. Different from both earlier videos (chalkboard, 3D studio) so the
channel does not repeat itself, but it keeps what the creator liked in Deadbeats: one colour of meaning per chapter,
real objects, graphs that move, a change every ≤ 3 s.

- Paper `#f4f0e7`, deep paper `#e7e0d1`, ink `#1c1a16`, soft ink `#6b655a`. Fine halftone dots (4 % ink) and grain.
- Type: Inter Tight 800 for numbers and key words (≤ 4 words per screen, always beside a visual).
  Newsreader for newspaper headlines and the quiet lines. No boxes, badges or eyebrow labels.

## Fact check (2026-11-21)

- Zuckerberg's $1 salary: from 2013 (Facebook proxy, 2013). Jobs: $1 a year from his 1997 return to Apple.
  Page and Brin: $1 from 2004–05. The script's "did the exact same thing" holds; the years differ, so a small
  credit says them.
- Top federal rate 37 %: holds. Long-term capital gains top rate 20 % + 3.8 % Net Investment Income Tax = 23.8 %:
  holds. No Social Security or Medicare payroll tax on capital gains: holds (the 3.8 % NIIT is a separate tax,
  already named in the script).
- "$100M salary, almost $50M gone": California illustration 37 % + 13.3 % state + 2.35 % Medicare ≈ 52 %. Holds as
  "almost half". Shown as an illustration.
- **Needs care:** "they take their compensation in stock". Stock *awards* (options, RSUs) are taxed as ordinary
  income when they vest or are exercised; only the growth after that is a capital gain. Zuckerberg, Page and Brin
  mostly own founder shares, which is why their gains are capital gains. The voice is recorded, so the line stays,
  with an on-screen note at 101.6 s: "Stock awards are taxed as income when they vest; later growth is a capital gain."
- Unrealized gains untaxed; securities-backed lines of credit (SBLOC) at low rates; loans are not income: all hold.
  Note on screen: "Loans are repaid with interest; rates vary."
- Selling shares gives up voting power: holds (Zuckerberg controls Meta through Class B shares).
