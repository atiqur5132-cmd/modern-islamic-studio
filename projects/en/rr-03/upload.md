📚 Sources and notes
• $1 salaries: Zuckerberg from 2013 (Facebook proxy statement, 2013; Meta still reports large "other compensation", mostly security costs). Steve Jobs from his 1997 return to Apple. Larry Page and Sergey Brin from 2004 (Google proxy statements).
• US federal tax rates (IRS): top ordinary income rate 37%; top long-term capital gains rate 20% for assets held more than a year; plus the 3.8% Net Investment Income Tax for high earners (23.8% in total).
• The $100M example is an illustration for a California top earner: 37% federal + 13.3% state + 2.35% Medicare (1.45% + 0.9% additional) ≈ 52.6%.
• Stock awards (options, RSUs) are taxed as ordinary income when they vest or are exercised; only the growth after that is a capital gain. Founders like Zuckerberg, Page and Brin mostly own founder shares.
• Unrealized gains are not taxed until the shares are sold.
• Securities-backed lines of credit (SBLOCs): rates vary; loans are repaid with interest, and a falling stock price can force a sale (a margin call). The strategy only works while the stock grows faster than the loan costs.
• Control: Zuckerberg controls Meta through Class B shares carrying 10 votes each.
