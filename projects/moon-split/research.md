Series A, Viral Claim Check, talking head (the faceless period is over). Same shape as the
flat-earth reel (~60k views in 2 days): claims from both sides, each checked, verdict
stamps. Researched 2026-11-09. The creator reads this before the script.

## The claims

1. **Muslim viral post:** "NASA ne maan liya, chaand do tukde hua tha", with an Apollo photo
   of a long crack on the Moon. Spread since 2007 when Dr Zaghloul El-Naggar used the photo
   (Digiteye, 14 Apr 2020).
2. **Critics:** "Agar chaand phata, toh duniya mein kisi aur ne dekha ya likha kyun nahi?"
3. **Indian legend:** a Kerala king, Cheraman Perumal, saw the moon split and went to Arabia.

## Tafsir: what scholars say

- **Ibn Kathir** on 54:1 (alim.org): "the scholars agree that the moon was cleft asunder
  during the lifetime of the Prophet"; calls the hadiths "authentic Mutawatir".
- **Maududi**, Tafhim: a real past event; rejects reading it as "the Moon will split".
- **Muhammad Asad**, note 1 on 54:1: most commentators see a past event, but he thinks the
  verse refers "to a future event", and suggests the reports may describe "an unusual kind
  of partial lunar eclipse"; says Zamakhshari reports the future reading had "the support of
  some of the earlier commentators".
- **al-Zajjaj** (via Mirqat on Bukhari 4864) mentions and rejects a group who read it as the
  Day of Resurrection.
- 54:2 "sihr mustamirr": "passing magic" (Mujahid, Qatadah via Ibn Kathir).

So: the majority view is a real event in the Prophet's ﷺ time; a minority (Asad, and some
early commentators per Zamakhshari) read the verse as about the end of time.

## NASA photo

- The photo: Apollo 10, 1969, AS10-30-4450 — Rima Ariadaeus. Wikimedia Commons file
  "Rima_Ariadaeus_as10-30-4450.jpg", CC0 (NASA original, public domain).
- What it is: "a linear rille", about 300 km long and 5 km wide, "thought to have been formed
  when a section of the Moon's crust sank down between two parallel fault lines"
  (Wikipedia, "Rima Ariadaeus"). Paul Groot (Radboud University): the "split" does not go
  round the whole Moon.
- NASA scientist Brad Bailey (NASA Lunar Science Institute, 21 June 2010), as quoted in
  Wikipedia "Splitting of the Moon" and "Rima Ariadaeus": "No current scientific evidence
  reports that the Moon was split into two (or more) parts and then reassembled at any point
  in the past", and "My recommendation is to not believe everything you read on the
  internet." The NASA page itself (sservi.nasa.gov) no longer shows the text; the archive
  copy could not be fetched. Quote through Wikipedia/Digiteye and say "2010 mein".

## Cheraman Perumal legend

- Wikipedia, "Splitting of the Moon", citing Sebastian Prange, *Monsoon Islam* (Cambridge,
  2018): Malabar Muslims "composed a story" in the 12th to 14th centuries CE; in it the king
  "had witnessed the Moon splitting in his dream", went to Arabia and died there; "Prange
  maintains that historical research has found this story to be fictitious."
- Not opened: Tuhfat al-Mujahidin (16th c.) and Qissat Shakarwati Farmad; the "629 CE
  Cheraman mosque" claim. Leave both out of the script.
