## The one idea

Your feed rewards you the way a slot machine does: sometimes yes, sometimes no, and you never
know which. That "shayad agla" is what the brain's learning system reacts to most, so the thumb
keeps going. 1400 years ago a hadith called free time a capital that most people trade away at a
loss (*maghbun*). The video does not say phones are poison; it says what is going on, and lets the
viewer decide what to buy with their time.

A 15-year-old could repeat it as: "Reels kabhi achhi, kabhi bekaar hoti hain, isliye hum ruk nahi
paate. Aur hadith kehti hai free time ek paisa hai jo log ghaate mein bech dete hain."

Picture to hold: a **trade receipt**. Every chapter adds a line to a receipt of what one evening of
scrolling cost and what it bought. The last chapter totals it, and the word *maghbun* (cheated in a
sale) is the stamp on it.

Checked 2026-11-13 (night of the build):
- Schultz's set-up: Schultz, "Dopamine reward prediction error coding", *Dialogues in Clinical
  Neuroscience* 18(1):23–32 (2016), PMC4826767: dopamine neurons respond "when a stimulus, such
  as a light, picture, or sound predicts a reward", "remain at baseline activity for fully
  predicted rewards", and the monkeys' rewards are fruit juices. Lines 49–51 hold.
- Pigeons on ratio schedules: pigeon key-pecking on variable-ratio schedules is standard (e.g.
  "Yoked variable-ratio and variable-interval responding in pigeons", JEAB, PMC1333627); secondary
  sources describe Ferster & Skinner (1957) as mostly pigeon work. The three-box scene stays
  tagged `Illustration`.

Earlier list (now settled as above):
